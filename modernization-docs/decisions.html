ADR-002 Keep LuaJIT 2.1 — do not migrate to Luau or Lua 5.4 Accepted

2026-05-16

Context

The engine is on LuaJIT 2.1 (Lua 5.1.4 dialect, frozen language). Luau (typed Lua) and Lua 5.4 are tempting modern targets.

Decision

Stay on LuaJIT 2.1. Bump it to the current rolling release for the CVE-2024-25176 fix, but keep the runtime.

Consequences

We retain JIT + FFI performance and do not fracture the existing mod ecosystem — ~53K LOC of community Lua is all 5.1-dialect. The real scripting liability is luabind, not LuaJIT (see ADR-003).

ADR-003 Replace luabind 0.7.1 with sol2/sol3 Proposed

2026-05-16 · dev-review correction (2026-05-17): the upstream lead confirms the sol3 branch compiles but is not functional — sol2 changes usertype property/constructor semantics (sol2 issue #1513) and breaks vanilla and modded Lua. Re-executing it as-is collides with ADR-004; the real cost is a compatibility shim. Reassess whether this migration is worth doing at all.

Context

Decision (proposed — pending an effort spike)

ADR-004 Modernization is incremental and mod-compatible by default Accepted

2026-05-16

Context

Upstream is a very small team with documented mod-compatibility paralysis; large breaking PRs sit for years and rot. We must not reproduce that failure mode.

Decision

Every change ships as a small, independently-mergeable unit. Any change to the mod-facing INI/Lua surface that breaks existing mods requires its own ADR and a migration path. [ARCH] items are sliced into shippable increments before work starts.

Consequences

Slower headline progress, but work actually lands and stays mergeable. This is the governing principle for the whole Roadmap.

ADR-005 Do not duplicate upstream's active branches Accepted

2026-05-16 · dev-review correction (2026-05-17): the upstream lead advises against a Box2D physics switch outright — CC's atom physics is core identity, the box2d-emscripten physics layer never matured, and a real port would need extensive CC-specific work. Context

Do not start competing implementations of Box2D physics, the next-gen renderer, or the web port. For physics multithreading (T3-01), parallelize the existing atom-travel loop — not a Box2D switch (see the dev-review correction above). We may revive stalled, un-owned branches (sol3, hot-reloading).

Consequences

Avoids wasted effort and merge conflicts. Focuses our work on genuinely un-owned gaps.

ADR-008 Fork-first development — upstreaming is opportunistic, never a gate Accepted

2026-05-16

Context

Upstream (CCCP) merges PRs very slowly — some sit open for years. Gating our progress on upstream review would stall the effort indefinitely.

Decision

cortex-modern is the primary product. All work happens continuously on the modernization-effort integration branch (features via short feature/* branches, merged in at our own review bar). development is kept as a clean mirror of upstream/development and is never committed to directly. Upstreaming is optional and downstream: to offer a feature we cut a pr/* branch from upstream/development, cherry-pick that feature's commits, and open a small self-contained PR — only when we choose to. We never block ongoing work waiting on upstream, and we sync upstream/development into the integration branch regularly to avoid drift.

Consequences

We are never blocked on the maintainers; upstream merges become a bonus, not a dependency. The cost is our own review discipline and periodic upstream syncs. This resolves the former "Upstreaming" open question.

ADR-010 Corefall cross-pollination posture — harvest the mod tier, leave the sim Accepted

2026-05-16

Context

A full read of Corefall's milestone specs (M1–M49), cross-checked against the Cortex Command engine source, produced 145 candidate ideas — see Corefall Ideas. ADR-007 deferred this decision until Corefall had a defined architecture; it now does.

Decision

Adopt Corefall ideas only as fresh, clean-room implementations, and concentrate on the moddable side. Pursue the [MOD] tier — AI/squad depth, causal damage and afflictions, loot and perks, factions, missions and storytellers, the traits/morale/memory colony layer — as ordinary Cortex Command .rte mods, forming a feature track parallel to the engine Roadmap. Do not adopt the [ARCH] deep-simulation pillars (atmospherics, material cellular-automata, thermal, vehicle/fluid physics) — they are Corefall's identity and rebuilding them here is wasted effort. The one enabling [CODE] investment worth making is widening the Lua API and adding a custom-property mechanism, which converts further clusters of ideas into pure mod work.

Consequences

cortex-modern gains a large, low-risk content backlog that needs no engine fork. The clean-room boundary is strict and one-directional: ideas may flow Corefall → cortex-modern, but no Cortex Command (AGPL) code or assets may ever enter Corefall (Apache/MIT). Extends and closes ADR-007.

ADR-012 The campaign — a committed strategic + tactical flagship Accepted

2026-05-17 · accepted (was Proposed, 2026-05-16)

Context

Cortex Command ships one campaign — "Conquest", the Metagame — and the shipping game's own notice (MainMenuGUI.cpp, ShowMetaGameNoticeScreen()) calls it "very INCOMPLETE and flawed", promises a ground-up rework that has no code behind it, and redirects players to the Void Wanderers mod. The campaign is the #2 community complaint after AI. With the AI overhaul (ADR-014) and Multiplayer (ADR-015) committed, the campaign was the last open flagship question.

A full investigation produced the Campaign page: a direct source read of the Metagame (rules welded into a ~6,000-line MetagameGUI, coin-flip auto-resolution, no persistence, no content format); a study of Void Wanderers, the community's de-facto campaign; the in-game lore and the Android Arts / Data fluff doc — which revealed a dev-canonical but unfinished story arc (the Bombies, the sentient Orange Dummy CPU, "Star System Death", Uzira, the Mu-ilaak finale) marked "Awaiting cleanup!" and never shipped; and the Corefall campaign design. Ten campaign designs were narrowed to six combined visions and ranked.

Decision

Make the campaign cortex-modern's third committed flagship. The chosen design is "The Frontier": a hybrid — Conquest's strategic war map, fixed and evolved, with a persistent tactical mercenary-company campaign hanging off it; a RimWorld-style storyteller pacing it; neutral factions (a five-step disposition track) and neutral bosses as first-class actors; and the game's own unfinished story, finished, as the anchor-mission spine. It is built as a new Data/Campaign.rte/ on the GAScripted + GlobalScript contract — overwhelmingly [MOD], no engine fork for layers L0–L5. The build is seven dependency-ordered milestones (M0–M6), agent-driven, gated by the CMP campaign-trust suite, no human-time estimates. Binding points: M0 extracts the metagame model out of MetagameGUI before anything else; M1 ships "Conquest, Rebuilt" as a standalone release that retires the in-game apology notice; the anchor missions use the dev-canonical lore; neutral factions and bosses are first-class; Void Wanderers is not forked — it is mined and kept green in the AI flagship's CI mod corpus; the grand-strategy depth is opt-in, off by default; progression is horizontal; the campaign save rides .ccsave on the determinism-island contract. The Campaign page is the living plan and the artifact an implementer builds from.

Consequences

The campaign is the capstone flagship — it consumes the other two. It rides ADR-014's order vocabulary, squad coordination, decision-event channel and replay (the AI makes its battles worth fighting); co-op campaign is Multiplayer's M4; and it shares the AI / Multiplayer M0 harness, the .ccsave path and the determinism contract — one harness, three flagships. It is mostly [MOD] and needs no engine fork. It finishes Cortex Command's scrapped story, which is the campaign's identity differentiator. Void Wanderers continues as a separate, CI-tracked mod. This supersedes ADR-012's own Proposed form and resolves the last open flagship decision; the six-vision analysis, the architecture, the milestones and the validation suite live on the Campaign page.

ADR-014 The AI overhaul — a committed, locomotion-first plan Accepted

2026-05-16 · re-scoped 2026-05-17 after upstream-dev (Causeless) review and a full read of the AI source. The original draft mis-diagnosed the AI — it proposed a layered-hybrid decision architecture (utility AI, HTN, influence maps, a D* Lite pathfinder rewrite) for what is really an execution problem, and it proposed navigation work the v7 development branch had already shipped. The decision below is the corrected, source-verified plan; the AI page is the living artifact.

Context

AI quality is the #1 community complaint and has been for twenty years; the upstream lead has openly called it "garbage". It is also the foundation under the other two flagship efforts — the Campaign is only as good as its battles, and co-op Multiplayer runs enemy AI server-side. A full read of the AI source (the ~5,830-line Lua AI plus the C++ pathfinder, controller and harness) and an upstream-dev review produced a verified diagnosis: the player-visible failures — actors flying into walls, jamming on terrain, killing themselves — are failures of execution (locomotion, path-following, stuck-recovery), not of high-level decision-making, which is adequate. The pathfinder is async, jump-aware and capable, and was recently overhauled; it is not the problem.

Decision

Make the AI overhaul cortex-modern's flagship and first major build. Approach: locomotion-first — improve the existing Lua AI rather than replace it. Rework the path-follower (GoToWpt), add diagnosed stuck-recovery and a hazard-reflex layer, build real squad coordination, and make a focused perception / weapon-choice / orders pass. The engine work is small and bounded: an emergency-interrupt, a danger query, a decision-event channel for observability, a wider order vocabulary. No pathfinder rewrite (MicroPather stays); no utility-AI / HTN / influence-map / behaviour-tree tower. Seven dependency-ordered milestones (M0–M6), beginning with the validation harness, gated by an automated trust suite. The build is agent-driven, sequenced by dependency and validation; "done" is the machine-measured definition on the AI page.

Consequences

The AI is the substrate beneath ADR-012 (the Campaign) and ADR-015 (Multiplayer) — both depend on competent AI. It is mostly [MOD] (Lua) work with a small set of [CODE] engine additions; MicroPather and the existing pathfinder are kept. T1-06 is superseded — it becomes the roadmap's pointer to this flagship rather than a standalone Tier-1 item. Existing mods keep working through a preserved compatibility surface (the command-API facade plus an opt-in legacy AI profile). The AI page is the living plan and the artifact an implementer builds from.

ADR-015 Multiplayer — server-authoritative state replication, staged Accepted

Sequencing amended by ADR-016 (2026-05-18): the architectural commitment in this ADR stands — per-mode netcode on a deterministic core. The order in which the foundation is built changed from M1 server spine + grow-the-island to determinism-first M1–M5 then per-mode adapters M6–M10. The text below is the historical 2026-05-17 record; see ADR-016 for the new sequence and rationale (Teardown recantation, corefall lock, Factorio precedent, performance-ceiling argument).

2026-05-16 · dev-review note (2026-05-17): the upstream lead's review finds full determinism materially more reachable than this decision first assumed — the RNG is constant-seeded, the threading has the SyncedUpdate boundary, modern FP is consistent. That re-prices the determinism retrofit (M5) and rollback PvP (M6) downward; it does not change the staged architecture — state-replication co-op still ships first, since it needs only the terrain island while lockstep needs the whole mod corpus deterministic. The M0 determinism spike measures the remaining cost. See the Multiplayer page.

Context

ADR-013 reopened the multiplayer question and commissioned the full option analysis on the Multiplayer page. Cortex Command's old networking — server-side screen-streaming over RakNet — was removed in January 2025 and cannot be revived: the integration hooks were stripped from five managers, and the per-player 8-bit render path it depended on is gone. The investigation — a direct read of the engine source and the removal history, the comparable games (Teardown, Terraria, Noita, Factorio), and Corefall's netcode design — produced a ranked seven-option solution space. The owner has committed to the recommended path, in full.

Decision

Make networked multiplayer cortex-modern's second committed flagship, after the AI overhaul. Architecture: server-authoritative state replication, staged. One authoritative simulation — a player's host or a headless cortex-server, built into the game and community-hostable; clients send input, predict their own actor, and reconcile. Terrain destruction is replicated as semantic commands over a determinism island, never as pixels. The work is sequenced as seven dependency-ordered milestones (M0–M6): a Steam Remote Play stopgap and the sync harness; the headless server spine; terrain replication; entity replication and co-op prediction; co-op shipped; the whole-tick determinism island; and rollback PvP. Lockstep / rollback is reached by growing the determinism island, not started cold. No human-time estimates — the build is agent-driven, sequenced by dependency and gated by an automated per-tick-checksum harness. The Multiplayer page is the living plan and the artifact an implementer builds from.

Consequences

Co-op ships at milestone M4; competitive PvP at M6. RakNet is retired at M1; the ~5,900 lines of removed networking code are kept as a reference until then, and T1-03 (delete the dead networking code) is absorbed into this flagship rather than actioned separately. The determinism retrofit — chiefly the Lua mod corpus and the threaded sim — is the plan's named risk and is isolated in M5; mod hash sync is mandatory. Multiplayer shares the AI overhaul's M0 observability harness and depends on competent server-side AI (ADR-014), so it is sequenced to ride that work. This resolves the multiplayer direction ADR-013 left open.

ADR-016 Multiplayer sequencing pivot — determinism foundation first, then per-mode netcode Accepted

2026-05-18

Context

ADR-015 committed to server-authoritative state replication with a growing determinism island (M1 server spine → M2 terrain → M3 entities → M4 co-op ship → M5 determinism → M6 rollback PvP). Deeper research surfaced three pieces of 2026 evidence that the staging optimizes for the wrong thing:

  • Teardown creator Dennis Gustafsson recanted publicly in the same blog post the wiki cited as architectural blueprint: "Early on, I dismissed the idea of full determinism for the entire engine (a view I have since reevaluated)" — the Teardown team is moving past their shipped hybrid.
  • The corefall sibling project locked the per-mode-netcode-on-deterministic-core architecture in DR-052 (2026-05-06). cortex-modern was the only one of the two projects deferring determinism.
  • Factorio's deterministic lockstep ships ~500 kbit/s/client at thousands of entities; the cap on actor count becomes the slowest client's CPU rather than one host's, uncapped when M4 multi-threads sim across cores.

Decision

Restructure the MP roadmap to determinism-first ordering with per-mode netcode on top. The new sequence is M1 pre-determinism cleanup → M2 LuaJIT deterministic iteration → M3 fixed-point atom physics → M4 multi-threaded sim with deterministic merge → M5 cross-platform / WASM canonical anchor → M6 server spine + GNS transport → M7 LAN lockstep SHIP (first user-visible — smallest adapter, proves the determinism foundation in real network play) → M8 online co-op SHIP (server-auth + client prediction + reconciliation) → M9 GGPO rollback PvP SHIP → M10 public hosting (stretch). First MP user-visible ship slips from ~M4 to ~M7 (~6 months); in exchange the performance ceiling on actors, physics, and terrain destruction becomes uncapped (every client runs the full multi-threaded sim in parallel), four MP modes ship instead of two, and the determinism foundation is permanent.

The ADR-015 architectural commitment stands — per-mode netcode (lockstep / prediction / rollback) on a deterministic core. ADR-016 changes the order in which the foundation under that architecture is built.

Consequences

M1–M5 are foundation work (no user-visible MP); M6 enables; M7–M9 ship MP modes; M10 is stretch. Every M1–M5 milestone delivers single-player value (better stability, deterministic replays, fixed-point terrain perf, ~1.2× sim throughput from threading (measured), WASM web build), so the pivot is value-positive at every stage even if MP work pauses. Pivot is reversible at any milestone — M1 work alone strengthens single-player; original ADR-015 ordering remains a viable fallback. See multiplayer.html §11 for the full reasoning.

Alternative considered: AI Controller-sync (each player runs AI locally; only Controllers traverse the network; sim must respond deterministically to Controllers, a strictly weaker requirement than full determinism) was surfaced in upstream-dev review (2026-05) after M1–M4 had shipped. It is a simpler architecture that fits server-auth modes (M8, M10) but cannot support LAN lockstep (M7) or GGPO rollback (M9). Rejected because we committed to all four modes; the counter-intuitive bandwidth analysis (lockstep ~30 kbit/s vs. Controller-sync ~3 Mbit/s at 200 actors) makes lockstep cheaper on the wire too. See multiplayer.html §11.7 for the full comparison including the constraint cost (no wall-clock / no env-seeded RNG / no ML inference inside the sim).

ADR-017 Engine modernization runs as parallel side tracks alongside MP Accepted

2026-05-18

Context

The MP main spine (M1–M10, per ADR-016) handles the determinism-critical engine work directly: the LuaJIT patch (M2), fixed-point atom physics (M3), deterministic threaded sim (M4), WASM anchor (M5), GNS transport (M6). But several engine modernization concerns sit outside the determinism island and don't gate MP shipping: render-frame-rate decoupling, the Camera/RenderBatch abstraction, GPU-accelerated cosmetic systems, comprehensive CLI test surface. Each of these has a substantial upstream branch (often dormant) that's worth mining: interpolated-render-redux (Causeless, active), gpu-renderer (HeliumAnt, dormant), box2d-emscripten (WASM half, dormant), the original interpolated-render (abandoned but useful reference).

Decision

Run engine modernization as five parallel side tracks alongside the MP main spine, each with explicit dependencies and "pairs with" milestones:

  • T-RENDER-INTERP — collaborate with Causeless on interpolated-render-redux. M1 Block C (stable MOID iteration) overlaps with his MOID-stability work; coordinate to avoid double-effort.
  • T-RENDER-GPU — harvest Camera + RenderBatch from HeliumAnt's gpu-renderer branch selectively (no wholesale merge; HeliumAnt is dormant).
  • T-WASM-BUILD — fused into M5; harvest the WASM build scaffold from box2d-emscripten (drop the Box2D physics half per ADR-005).
  • T-COSMETIC — GPU offload of particle / post-process / audio mixing systems; lands alongside M7 so first MP ship benefits from the CPU savings.
  • T-VALIDATION — cccp-ctl CLI test surface modeled on corefall's cfctl spec; grows incrementally with each MP milestone.

None of these side tracks gate MP. If a side track stalls, MP continues. Each side track delivers independent single-player value when it lands. Full plan, timeline visualization, what-ships-when table, risk/fallback paths, and coordination strategy live in engine.html.

Consequences

The engineering effort can run multiple workstreams in parallel without blocking. MP main spine is well-defined (M1–M10 per ADR-016). Side tracks can be picked up by other contributors (or AI agents) when the MP main spine is between deliverables. Causeless's active branch is treated as a coordination opportunity (talk before duplicating); HeliumAnt's dormant branch is a fixed artifact to mine. ADR-005 (no Box2D physics switch) is honored — only the WASM half of box2d-emscripten is harvested.

ADR-018 MP architecture flips to Controller-sync — ADR-016's lockstep rejection reversed Accepted

2026-05-25 · amends ADR-016 §7.7 alternative-considered: the Controller-sync alternative that ADR-016 rejected (because it can't support LAN lockstep or GGPO rollback) is now adopted as the MP architecture after upstream-lead clarification.

Context

ADR-016 chose determinism-first + per-mode netcode (lockstep / prediction / rollback) on a fully deterministic core. The "Alternative considered" section evaluated Controller-sync (each player runs AI locally; only Controllers traverse the network; sim must respond deterministically to Controllers but AI itself doesn't need to) and rejected it because it cannot support LAN lockstep (M7) or GGPO rollback (M9).

Causeless's 2026-05-25 Discord clarification reframes the rationale: "the reasoning behind the Controller-sync idea is more for allowing expensive operations (like pathfinding requests) to be async. Async operations are pretty much fundamentally opposed to determinism — but if we add a boundary between the calculation and the simulator-y execution, then we have something deterministic." The Controller boundary is the deterministic boundary; async pathfinding stays async per-machine.

Under Controller-sync, lockstep IS still possible — the sync unit is the Controller (compact per-tick player intent state), not full sim state. Each machine runs its own deterministic sim downstream of received Controllers, with its own (non-deterministic) AI generating its own actors' Controllers. The per-machine AI can use async pathfinding without breaking the determinism contract because pathfinding outputs only reach the cross-machine sync via Controllers, which are compact + deterministic.

Decision

Flip to Controller-sync MP architecture from M7 onward. ADR-016's "Alternative considered" Controller-sync rejection is reversed; the determinism-first foundation work stands but the per-mode netcode (M7 lockstep, M8 prediction+reconciliation, M9 rollback) is rebuilt on the Controller-sync boundary instead of full sim sync. Specifically:

  • M4A pathfinder serial-epilogue is NEVER reapplied to revamp trunks — Controller-sync replaces the cross-machine sync requirement that motivated it. Each machine runs its own async pathfinder per-machine.
  • M4A Race A architectural fix is NEVER reapplied — same reason.
  • M7 (LAN lockstep) syncs Controllers per tick, not arbitrary sim state. Controllers are the deterministic input; sim downstream of Controllers is deterministic via M4A's Path E enforcement.
  • M8 (online rollback co-op) implements separate AI Lua states (Causeless's stated design intent fully realized): AI runs on each player's machine for their own actors in a non-deterministic AI Lua pool; sim Lua remains deterministic; only the resulting Controllers sync. Block E of M8.
  • M9 (online rollback PvP) reuses M8's rollback machinery on the Controller-sync boundary.

Consequences

The MP architecture matches Causeless's stated upstream design intent precisely — what M7+ ships is upstream-mergeable in principle. The pathfinder serial-epilogue (which would have been load-bearing under ADR-016's full-sim-sync model) is dropped — smaller engine diff, better perf, less invasive sim change. The M9 PvP rollback ship is still the project's stated north star; only the route changes. ADR-019 Clean stage trunks — rebuild foundation on determinism-revamp per upstream-lead review Accepted

2026-05-25 · strategic restructuring of how the work is organized in response to Causeless's 2026-05-25 Discord review of modernization-effort.

Context

Causeless's 2026-05-25 review surfaced three concerns about the work as it stood on modernization-effort:

  1. Bundled commits mix unrelated efforts. The M5.5 cccp-ctl branch alone bundled test infrastructure + TSan CI + race fixes + cross-actor enforcement + macOS UB fixes — five logically separate concerns in one branch. His verbatim feedback: "AI is very good at creating enormous quantities of code (often far in excess of any provable value therein) — they're far easier to review/merge/understand if they're smaller and more well-defined PRs."
  2. Architectural decisions rejected. M3 Q40.24 fixed-point math: "I don't see really no benefit for it from my brief 5 minutes thinking about it — if Lua float math is non-deterministic, then the game is non-deterministic regardless of how you handle the physics." His preferred cross-platform path is FPU + libm standardization + -ffp-contract=off, not fixed-point.
  3. Controller-sync MP rationale clarified. Async pathfinding incompatibility with determinism is the real motivation; the Controller boundary solves it. See ADR-018.

The user's strategic choice was: continue accumulating on modernization-effort (paying a continuous "clean it up later for upstream" tax that may never get paid) OR restructure with clean stage trunks (one-time rebuild cost; clean diffs from then on; upstream-mergeable in principle).

Decision

Restructure the work onto clean stage trunks, leaving modernization-effort preserved as legacy reference:

  • Stage 1: determinism-revamp — cut fresh from upstream/development (current tip). Foundation rebuild via 7 PRs across 4 waves (consolidated 2026-05-26 PM from earlier 22-24-PR iteration); one conceptual unit per PR with 2-8 internal commits inside for bisectability. PRs: vendor (BLAKE3 + nlohmann/json), cross-platform stability (macOS UB + Race B atomic + libm flags), M0+M1 determinism foundation (SimChecksum + RNG split + TimerMan + island leaks), Path E enforcement (full M4 Block B/C/D/E underneath), M2 Lua determinism (pairs + math.random per Lua state + os stubs + CI gate), cccp-ctl test harness + scenarios, CI gates (TSan + cross-platform validation). Drops M3 Q40.24, M4A pathfinder-epilogue, M4A Race A architectural fix, multi-platform race-investigation iteration commits, M5.5 mega-bundling. Detail: D:/Projects/DETERMINISM_REVAMP_PLAN.md.
  • Stage 2: multiplayer-revamp — cut from Stage 1 tip when Stage 1 closes. 80+ smaller per-concern PRs decomposing M6–M10 milestones. Controller-sync MP architecture from day one (ADR-018). Detail: D:/Projects/MULTIPLAYER_REVAMP_PLAN.md.
  • Stage 0 (legacy): modernization-effort — preserved as-is. Source-of-truth for code being selectively brought forward. In-flight stragglers (e.g., Race D Windows round-3) can finish here. No new milestone work cuts from it. No destructive operations on the branch or its history.

Every commit on a revamp trunk is one Causeless could read and either merge or critique cleanly. Smaller PR cadence is mandatory across both stages.

Consequences

The "clean it up later for upstream" tax is avoided by not creating it. Upstream merge becomes feasible in principle for each commit on revamp trunks. Wall-clock cost: ~5–7 days for Stage 1 foundation rebuild (7 consolidated PRs across 4 waves). The end goal (working PvP rollback MP) is unchanged; the route is via clean trunks instead of accumulating on the legacy trunk. Causeless's three feedback items all addressed: smaller well-defined PRs (7 cohesive PRs, each one conceptual unit a reviewer can hold in their head, with 2-8 internal commits inside for bisectability), Q40.24 dropped (FPU + libm path validated empirically at Stage 1 PR #7), Controller-sync MP from M7 onward (per ADR-018). The CLAUDE.md "Stage convention" + "Architecturally rejected" + "PR decomposition" sections capture the binding rules.

ADR-020 Fork-first execution discipline — Stage 1 runs on the fork; upstream PRs are opportunistic, never blocking Accepted

2026-05-27 · operational discipline that makes ADR-008's "fork-first; upstreaming opportunistic, never a gate" stance literal for Stage 1 execution.

Context

ADR-008 (fork-first development) was the standing policy from day one of the cortex-modern fork: "work in cortex-modern first; optionally upstream via pr/* branches later." In practice, the upstream LuaJIT PRs (PR A = upstream #279, PR B held back pending PR A merge) drifted toward being implicit gates on Stage 1: the question "can we start Stage 1 Wave 1?" kept getting answered with "after PR A + PR B land upstream." That implicit gate compounded with a review-pace observation that crystalized the issue on 2026-05-27: upstream PR #279, opened 2026-05-26 PM, had received no review after 24+ hours, while prior PRs against the user's fork branches had been reviewed near-instantly. The contrast surfaced a broader risk — if Stage 1's 7 PRs each waited on Causeless's organic review pace, Stage 1 wall-clock would balloon 5–10× from the planned ~5–7 days.

The user's choice was: (a) wait for Causeless and accept the slowdown, (b) burn the upstream bridge and fork fully away, or (c) enforce ADR-008 strictly — ship to the fork at the user's own pace, keep upstream PRs open as goodwill, and let upstream submissions be a per-PR opportunistic decision rather than a sequencing gate. (c) is what ADR-008 always said; it just hadn't been treated as binding.

Decision

Adopt fork-first execution as the binding operational discipline for Stage 1 and beyond. Concretely:

  • All Stage 1 work lands on Madreag/cortex-modern:determinism-revamp first. Every Stage 1 PR (Pre-Wave-1 LuaJIT folding + Waves 1-4's 7 PRs) is cut as a sub-branch from determinism-revamp, opened as a fork PR against Madreag/cortex-modern:determinism-revamp, reviewed by the user (with AI review agents per PR for substantive coverage), and merged at the user's pace.
  • Upstream submission is per-PR opportunistic. When Causeless is responsive, mirror the PR upstream via an pr/<name>-upstream branch cut from upstream/development directly. When he's not, fork-only is acceptable.
  • LuaJIT modernization (PR A + PR B) merged into determinism-revamp via the Pre-Wave-1 procedure (DETERMINISM_REVAMP_PLAN.md §5.0): merge pr/luajit-update-current-tip into determinism-revamp (fast-forward), then merge pr/luajit-windows-source-build (5 conflicts resolved in the merge commit using the tested recipe). ~10 min total. The PR branches themselves are unchanged — same branches stay ready for upstream submission whenever appropriate. Gives the fork the LuaJIT modernization regardless of upstream pace.
  • Upstream PRs stay open as goodwill. PR #279 remains open; PR B's upstream branch stays held back, rebases and opens upstream after #279 merges. No bridge burned; future cooperation preserved.
  • Patience timeline per upstream PR (applies to PR #279 and any future upstream submission): day 0–7 organic review; day 7 polite ping if silent; day 14–21 close-and-fold the upstream PR amicably if no engagement. The work is already in determinism-revamp (via Pre-Wave-1 or the corresponding fork merge), so closing an unanswered upstream PR has no effect on fork progress — it's purely a goodwill signal. For PR #279 opened 2026-05-26 PM: ping 2026-06-02, close-and-fold 2026-06-09 to 2026-06-16.
  • Stage exit criteria are evaluated on the fork only. Stage 1 closes when determinism-revamp meets DETERMINISM_REVAMP_PLAN.md §9 criteria. Upstream merge status of any PR is explicitly NOT an exit criterion.

Consequences

Stage 1 velocity decouples from upstream review pace. The 7-PR plan plus Pre-Wave-1 LuaJIT folding executes at the user's chosen cadence (~5–7 days agent work + verification), independent of whether Causeless engages with any individual upstream PR. The M9 north star (working PvP rollback MP) is reachable on this fork regardless of upstream merge status. Each fork PR is still STRUCTURED to be upstream-friendly — same commit-message discipline, same Causeless-style PR body, same scope limits — so opportunistic upstream submission of any fork PR is a cherry-pick + push, not a rewrite. Cost: slight duplication for the dual-track PRs (upstream-bound PRs need a separate pr/<name>-upstream branch from upstream/development); ongoing decision overhead per PR ("upstream this one too?"). Both are small relative to the 5–10× wall-clock cost of gating on Causeless's pace.

Alternatives rejected

  • Wait for Causeless: 5–10× wall-clock multiplier on Stage 1. M9 ship date slips by months for no quality gain.
  • Burn the upstream bridge (close PR #279, never submit upstream again): premature; no upside vs keeping PRs open; loses future cooperation; signals bad-faith to the upstream community.
  • Combine PRs into mega-bundles for faster review: directly contradicts Causeless's 2026-05-25 feedback (rejected the m5.5 mega-bundle pattern). Same trap that ADR-019 addresses.
  • Demand SLA on upstream reviews: not a thing in open source; would damage the relationship.
ADR-021 The determinism solution, finalized (r7) — cosmetic-RNG root cause, STRID=0 over det_pairs(), built-in hash over BLAKE3 Accepted

2026-06-05 · the empirical close of the determinism foundation, verified cross-platform GREEN on the reference branch fix/determinism-r7 (Windows + Linux + macOS).

Context

Stage 1's determinism work (ADR-019) bottomed out in a five-round cross-platform root-cause hunt. The decisive finding inverts the original mental model: it was never a threading problem. The divergence was a family of host-dependent cosmetic-RNG leaks — render, audio and effect-rotation code drawing the sim RNG (g_SimRNG) on host-frame-rate-gated frames, the audio path (SoundSet::SelectNextSounds + pitch, where FMOD's host-dependent channel gating varied the draw count) being the dominant multi-core offender — plus two uninitialized pooled reads (Atom::Clear leaving m_IntPos and Bresenham step state unset). Critically, Windows' scheduler hid the multi-core divergence: every Windows-only "GREEN" was a false-GREEN, and the Linux/macOS --threads 1,2,4,8 matrix is the real proof (necessary-but-not-sufficient on Windows). The fix was made with full parallelism intact — a standing project rule, never serialize the race away. An r7 finalization pass then revisited three design choices that had accreted during the hunt.

Decision

Adopt the r7 solution as the determinism foundation, with three corrections to the earlier in-flight design:

  • Drop the global det_pairs() Lua replacement → stock pairs() + LUAJIT_SECURITY_STRID=0. String-keyed table iteration is deterministic because STRID=0 makes LuaJIT assign string-ids in allocation order (deterministic) rather than randomised, and table string-key order is the string-id (verified from lj_tab.h: hashstr(t,s)=hashmask(t,(s)->sid)). The old det_pairs() sorted keys on every iteration yet its comparator punted on pointer / table / function / userdata keys (compared them equal) — so it never fixed the real object-keyed case while taxing every loop. STRID=0 is the correct, zero-cost mechanism; object-keyed sim tables key by UniqueID instead.
  • Drop the BLAKE3 crypto vendor → a small built-in FNV-1a / splitmix64 integer hash inside SimChecksum. BLAKE3 was crypto overkill for a divergence checksum; the built-in hash is integer-only, so it is bit-identical cross-platform by construction, and it removes a ~2,590-line vendor dependency.
  • Keep nlohmann/json — but scoped strictly to the structured test-report / trace format (the determinism harness's output, four tooling files), never the engine sim. Hand-rolling a JSON writer is the reinvent trap; this is the one vendor that earns its place.

Verified on Windows + Linux + macOS: the self-test positive control diverges as designed, single-core scenarios all match, and the --threads 1,2,4,8 matrix (including the M2PairsStress / M2LuaBaseline string-key canaries) is bit-identical multi-core == single-core. STRID=0 was confirmed reaching lj_str.c's compile.

Consequences

The determinism foundation is empirically closed and equivalent across all three platforms. The chosen cross-platform path validates ADR-019's bet (FPU + libm, no Q40.24): the fix lived in RNG-stream hygiene and uninitialised reads, not in the number system, so the Q40.24 rejection holds. Two vendor dependencies shrink to one. The mod-facing surface is untouched — pairs() behaves exactly as before for mods; only the build flag changes. The Windows-false-GREEN lesson is now a hard verification rule: a determinism claim is only proven on the Linux/macOS thread matrix, never on Windows alone. This decision records the what and why; the per-PR landing of these corrections is ADR-022. Full record: D:/Projects/R7_RESULTS.md and the five-round hunt log in D:/Projects/DETERMINISM_FIX_PACKAGING_PLAN.md.

ADR-023 The determinism boundary excludes AI output; M4ThreadStress's thread matrix is advisory by design Accepted

2026-06-08 · refines ADR-018 (Controller-sync) by naming what is, and is not, on the deterministic boundary.

Context

PR 10's longer-horizon CI surfaced an M4ThreadStress determinism residual (the scenario was renamed ThreadStress in the 2026-06-08 readiness fix round): at high thread counts the traces diverge starting ~tick 795 (run-to-run and across thread counts). It was first read as a re-cut regression and disproven — git diff stage/cumulative-verify..cd3f34afd is only the inert macOS Main.cpp guard, so the binaries are byte-identical on Linux. Root-causing it on Linux: the divergence is AI-led — the controller subsystem diverges first @795, with the physics subsystems (actors/particles/carve_math) following 2–3 ticks later as a downstream consequence of the different controller inputs. AI randomness is ruled out as the cause: every scripted hook is wrapped in a per-MO RNG scope (DeterministicMORNGScope), so math.random and the C++ sim RNG draw from a per-MO generator seeded by uniqueID+tick+phase — thread-invariant. What remains is a cross-actor read during the parallel AI (UpdateControllers runs the AI in a parallelize_loop over Lua states) whose value depends on how the loop partitioned the actors — i.e. on the thread count. The physics core itself is count-deterministic: M1/M2/M3 @900t are bit-identical across thread counts; only the threaded-AI-stress scenario diverges. The dropped FreezeStateForAIPhase (the “Frozen*” snapshot) would have made these reads order-independent, but it is architecturally rejected.

Decision

Name the deterministic boundary explicitly. Under Controller-sync (ADR-018) the AI runs per-machine and is not on the deterministic wire — only Controllers cross the network, and rollback records-and-replays Controllers rather than re-running the AI. Therefore cross-thread / cross-machine AI non-determinism is acceptable and out of scope for the determinism foundation. What must be deterministic — and is, verified — is the simulation given the same Controllers (the physics core, bit-identical across thread counts for M1/M2/M3). M4ThreadStress's thread-count matrix tests AI determinism, so it ships advisory in the CI (PR 10 already does this), documented as advisory-by design rather than a known-failing gate. We do not reintroduce FreezeStateForAIPhase/Frozen*, and we do not serialize the AI to force determinism — both fight the architecture for a property the goal does not need.

Consequences

Stage 1 is closeable: the deterministic boundary (sim given Controllers) is met and verified, and a future contributor who sees “M4ThreadStress thread-matrix diverges” finds this ADR instead of re-chasing it as a bug (as the PR-10 agent initially did). One narrow, separate item was checked and closed: under ThreadSanitizer the ≥16-thread races are LuaJIT-runtime internals (JIT mcode allocation + the allocator's mmap-hint global, rooted in lj_*) fired by concurrent Lua states — already classified as phantom noise and suppressed (race:^lj_ in tsan-known-races.md). They are address-level, benign for determinism values, and not a game-logic race, so there is nothing to surgically fix. If a future design ever needs deterministic AI replay (it does not under Controller-sync), this is the decision that would be revisited. Record: task #98, D:/Projects/PR10_CI_RESULTS.md, and the project-old-determinism-solution-to-reconcile memory.

ADR-024 Stage 2 multiplayer ships lockstep first; rollback is gated by measurement Accepted

2026-06-16 · supersedes rollback-first sequencing in ADR-015/016/018-era milestone docs while preserving Controller-sync.

Context

The previous Stage 2 docs made M8/M9 rollback the mandatory online path. That overcommits before the engine has the pieces rollback needs: complete snapshot/restore, fast re-sim, Lua/mod state boundaries, and audio/VFX side-effect suppression. At the same time, pure lockstep has real latency limits on high RTT or jittery links. Corefall-style server-authoritative architecture remains useful research, but porting that shape directly into Cortex Command first would turn the first playable milestone into a much larger state-replication project.

Decision

Make D:/Projects/MULTIPLAYER_BEST_PLAN.md the Stage 2 source of truth. Rollback-first language in M8_PLAN.md, M9_PLAN.md, MULTIPLAYER_REVAMP_PLAN.md, and older wiki sections is historical.
